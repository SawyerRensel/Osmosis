## Dry stores

- Rice, two kilos in a sealed tub
- Pasta, four packets
- Rolled oats for the morning watch
- Flour, enough for two loaves a week
- Sugar in a screw-top jar
- Salt and pepper
- Dried beans, soaked the night before
- Lentils
- Couscous, because it needs no simmering in a seaway
- Crackers in their own tin so they stay crisp
- Coffee, ground, plus a spare bag
- Tea bags
- Cocoa for night watches
- Powdered milk
- Stock cubes
- Dried fruit
- Nuts, shelled
- Honey in a squeeze bottle

## Tins

1. Tomatoes, chopped
2. Chickpeas
3. Sweetcorn
4. Tuna in oil
5. Sardines
6. Coconut milk
7. Peaches in juice
8. Condensed milk
9. Soup, the thick kind

11. Spare tin opener taped to the locker lid
12. Mark every tin's contents on its lid with a pen, in case the labels soak off in the bilge

## Fresh food

- Buy it the day before casting off
	- Fruit that keeps
		- Apples
		- Oranges
		- Lemons
	- Vegetables that keep
		- Potatoes in a dark locker
		- Onions in a net
		- Cabbage, whole
		- Carrots
	- Eat these first
		- Bread
		- Salad leaves
		- Soft cheese
- Turn the eggs every other day so the yolks stay central
- Keep the cool box shut while under way
- Check the fresh locker every morning and use whatever is turning first

## Water

- Fill both tanks at the berth
- Carry ten litres in cans as a reserve
- Note the tank gauge in the log at each watch change
- Use seawater for washing up when under way
- Rinse with fresh water only at the end
- Ration drinking water if the passage runs long, see [[Passage Plan]]
- Keep a hand pump for when the electric one fails
- Clean the tank filter every month

## Cooking gear

### Pots and pans

- Pressure cooker
- Deep frying pan with a lid
- Kettle with a whistle
- Saucepan, large
- Saucepan, small
- Oven tray that fits the gimballed oven

### Tools

- Sharp knife in its own sheath
- Chopping board with a non-slip mat
- Wooden spoons
- Ladle
- Tongs
- Grater
- Measuring jug
- Bottle opener
- Lighter and spare matches in a sealed bag

### Safety

- Galley strap to lean against in a seaway
- Fire blanket within reach of the stove
- Pan clamps on the cooker rail
- Oven gloves, long ones
- Gas alarm tested before each passage

## Stove

- Turn the gas on at the bottle, then at the cooker
- Light the burner straight away
- Never leave a lit burner unattended
- Turn the gas off at the bottle after every meal
- Check the hose for cracks each month
- Carry a spare bottle, full, strapped in the gas locker
- Keep the locker drain clear so leaked gas runs overboard

## Cleaning

- Washing-up liquid that works in seawater
- Scourers
- Tea towels, at least six
- Bin bags
- Keep food waste in a sealed tub until port
- Wipe the stove after every meal
- Clear the sink drain weekly
- Air the lockers on a dry day
- Check for damp behind the cool box

## Menus

1. Day one: the stew made at home, reheated
2. Day two: pasta with tinned tomatoes and tuna
3. Day three: rice, beans and sweetcorn
4. Day four: soup with fresh bread if there is any left
5. Day five: couscous with chickpeas and dried fruit
6. Day six: potato hash with onions and whatever needs eating
7. Day seven: pancakes, because it has been a long week
	- Flour, powdered milk and water
	- Honey on top
8. Spare day: anything from the tins
9. Storm day: cold food that needs no stove, planned with [[Weather]]
10. Arrival: eat ashore

## Night watches

- Fill a flask before dark
- Leave biscuits where the watch can reach them
- Keep a red torch by the galley
- Soup in a mug is easier than a bowl
- Wake the next watch with a hot drink
